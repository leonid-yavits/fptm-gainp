We test how the \wosnew{accuracy-oriented} ALeRCE comparison, the 20-clause FPTM against the 600-clause $\LF=1$ model, depends on the modelling choices.
At the 16~nm reference points, the strict model needs \hyp{1.51$\times$} (system point) and \hyp{1.54$\times$} (circuit point) the energy of the FPTM model. These ratios use the same assumed energy model as the 7~nm values.
At the predicted 7~nm corners the ratio is \hyp{1.55$\times$} (conservative) and \hyp{1.51$\times$} (optimistic).
If every cycle is charged one cell operation per column, regardless of the number of unmasked rows, the ratio becomes \hyp{1.28$\times$} at the conservative corner.
The ranking therefore holds for both energy models tested.
The controller share of the Mapping~S energy is \hyp{3\% to 4\%} on NSL-KDD, \hyp{14\% to 17\%} on ALeRCE and \hyp{41\% to 54\%} on WOS-46985 at the conservative corner. At the optimistic corner it is \hyp{13\% to 17\%}, \hyp{41\% to 45\%} and \hyp{69\% to 84\%}, because the array energy shrinks more than the upper-estimate controller energy. Without the controller, the strict-to-FPTM energy ratio on ALeRCE is \hyp{1.55$\times$}. With a five times lower sequencer and model-read energy, it is \hyp{1.55$\times$}, and with a 64-subarray macro it is \hyp{1.53$\times$}.
With a $10\times$ shorter retention time, the refresh overhead of the FPTM batch latency rises from \hyp{1.4\%} to \hyp{14.3\%} at the conservative corner. Its energy per sample rises from \hyp{4.42~nJ} to \hyp{5.90~nJ}.
Static energy is a large share wherever logical columns are tall and programs are long. For the 100-clause WOS-46985 FPTM it is \hyp{25\%} of the energy at the conservative corner, against \hyp{13\%} for the 200-clause $\LF=1$ model.
The refresh model assumes that the subarrays of a logical column refresh in lockstep through their local sense amplifiers (Section~\ref{sec:method}). If refresh instead used the shared sense amplifier, the $h$ subarrays would be refreshed one after another, and each refresh would stall the array for $h\times512$ cycles. This stall would take \hyp{14.3\%} (conservative) and \hyp{9.7\%} (optimistic) of every refresh period on ALeRCE ($h=10$), and \hyp{2.9\%} and \hyp{1.9\%} on NSL-KDD ($h=2$). On WOS-46985 ($h=79$) it would take \hyp{113\%} and \hyp{76\%} of the period, so Mapping~S would not be feasible at the conservative corner. Mapping~C on WOS-46985 stacks up to 79 subarrays and would be affected in the same way. All ALeRCE models use the same $h$ in Mapping~S, so their ranking would not change.
